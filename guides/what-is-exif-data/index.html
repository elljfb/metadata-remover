Photo privacy guide

What is EXIF data and why does it matter?

EXIF (Exchangeable Image File Format) is a set of details that a camera or phone may store in an image file when a photo is taken. It describes the image and how it was captured; it is separate from the visible pixels that make up the picture.

What can EXIF data include?

  • Capture date and time, exposure, ISO, aperture, and flash settings.
  • Camera or phone make and model, lens information, and sometimes a device serial number.
  • GPS latitude, longitude, and altitude, if location tagging was enabled.
  • Editing software, author or copyright information, and other metadata such as XMP.
  • An embedded thumbnail or preview image.

Not every photo contains every field. The available information depends on the device, camera settings, file format, and editing or sharing steps.

Why should you care about photo metadata?

Location and capture details can reveal where or when a photo was made, while device and editing details can disclose information you did not intend to share. That can matter when posting publicly, listing an item for sale, or sharing images of your home or routine.

Removing metadata helps reduce those embedded details, but it does not make a photo anonymous. Addresses, faces, documents, reflections, and other clues visible in the image remain. Review the picture itself before sharing.
